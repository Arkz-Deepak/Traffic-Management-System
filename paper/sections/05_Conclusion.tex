\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{AURA} (\textit{Adaptive Urban Routing and Actuation}), a decentralized traffic signal control architecture formulated as a Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) with action-duration bounds for resilient traffic regulation and emergency vehicle preemption. By bounding action durations between statutory limits ($10\,\text{s} \le \tau \le 45\,\text{s}$), enforcing deterministic 3-second yellow clearance intervals, and optimizing policy parameters with continuous-time discounting $\gamma^\tau$, AURA eliminates phase flickering and starvation hazards endemic to unconstrained reinforcement learning controllers while guaranteeing compliance with municipal traffic engineering standards.

Rigorous microscopic evaluations within Eclipse SUMO under the Krauss car-following model across both saturated peak-hour corridors and an authentic 177-intersection OpenStreetMap network of Midtown Manhattan confirmed the empirical effectiveness, statistical stability, and topological scalability of the proposed framework. Multi-seed evaluations over five distinct random seeds demonstrated that AURA consistently outperforms established adaptive and contemporary DRL baselines, reducing average halting queues to $15.81 \pm 1.53$ vehicles and slashing cumulative vehicular $\text{CO}_2$ emissions by up to 11.8\%. On the real Manhattan network under 1,501 circulating vehicles, AURA delivered the lowest overall average network queue ($\bar{Q} = 109.46$ vehicles) and the lowest peak network queue ($Q_{\max} = 257$ vehicles), outperforming Traditional Fixed-Time ($\bar{Q} = 126.73$, a 13.6\% reduction), Demand-Responsive Actuation ($\bar{Q} = 119.10$), and modern SOTA PressLight ($\bar{Q} = 116.72$). Crucially, under emergency preemption, AURA's localized rolling preemption accelerated emergency transit by 65.9\% without triggering network-wide gridlock cascades.

Furthermore, empirical compute profiling verified that AURA's decentralized formulation decouples computational complexity to $\mathcal{O}(1)$ per junction: requiring merely 18.52\,KB of parameter memory and 33.28\,$\mu$s CPU inference per decision (30,050 decisions/second/core), establishing that an entire 10,000-intersection statewide or metropolitan network can be coordinated in real time on less than 33\% of a single consumer laptop CPU core without server-farm infrastructure.

Future work will investigate hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) physical edge deployment across NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano modules. We intend to integrate edge acoustic siren detection and vision-based vehicle classification within a decentralized ROS~2 DDS peer-to-peer framework to evaluate multi-modal preemption triggers in live physical intersections.
