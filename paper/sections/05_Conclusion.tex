\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{AURA} (\textit{Adaptive Urban Routing and Actuation}), a decentralized traffic signal control architecture formulated as a Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) with action-duration bounds for resilient traffic regulation and emergency vehicle preemption. By bounding action durations between statutory limits ($10\,\text{s} \le \tau \le 45\,\text{s}$), enforcing deterministic 3-second yellow clearance intervals, and optimizing policy parameters with continuous-time discounting $\gamma^\tau$, AURA eliminates phase flickering and starvation hazards endemic to unconstrained reinforcement learning controllers while guaranteeing compliance with municipal traffic engineering standards.

Rigorous microscopic evaluations within Eclipse SUMO under the Krauss car-following model across both saturated peak-hour corridors and an authentic 177-intersection OpenStreetMap network of Midtown Manhattan confirmed the empirical effectiveness and topological scalability of the proposed framework. On the real Manhattan network under 1,501 circulating vehicles, AURA delivered the lowest overall average network queue ($\bar{Q} = 109.46$ vehicles) and the lowest peak network queue ($Q_{\max} = 257$ vehicles), outperforming Traditional Fixed-Time ($\bar{Q} = 126.73$, a 13.6\% reduction), Demand-Responsive Actuation ($\bar{Q} = 119.10$), and modern SOTA PressLight ($\bar{Q} = 116.72$). Crucially, under emergency preemption, AURA's localized rolling preemption accelerated emergency transit by 65.9\% (15\,s vs. 44\,s under fixed-time) without triggering network-wide gridlock cascades.

Future work will investigate hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) physical edge deployment across NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano modules. We intend to integrate edge acoustic siren detection and vision-based vehicle classification within a decentralized ROS~2 DDS peer-to-peer framework to evaluate multi-modal preemption triggers in live physical intersections.
